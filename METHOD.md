# Building a Fantasy Language in Seven easy Steps

> **NOTE:** *This is a language created using a video I found on YouTube that explained the concept well.*

The original video can be found [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0usHDqGgQAk).

## Step 0: Gather existing words

> Gather words already used in the text, e.g. for my HHG Standard Language, I already had *Cillientho* and *Sirnyas* existing before the first draft of this conlang.

---

## Step 1: Language Goals

> List everything you want this language to be, preferably in a short, meaningful paragraph of about three sentences.

---

## Step 2: Phonology

> Go through the English (or your preferred language) alphabet and pick out the sounds you want.
> 
> Once you have a list of all the alphabet you want, start making consonant (or vowel) pairs and define sounds from them.
> 
> **Pro Tip:** You can use the [IPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet) for sounds to choose from.

---

## Step 3: Grammar and Syntax

> **Weird Quirks.** Decide on stuff your language doesn't have in common with other languages.
> 
> **Word Order.** Decide on where to place *Subjects*, *Verbs*, and the two types of *objects*: direct objects and indirect objects.
> 
> **Adjectives and/or adverbs.** Decide how you can have modifiers on nouns or verbs, is it cases, affixes, words, etc.
> 
> **Affixes.** Decide how affixes apply to nouns, and what they do there.
> 
> **Articles.** Okay, you need at least two: a definite (*the* in English), and an indefinite (*a/an* in English).
> You can have more, of course. 
> 
> **Plural and/or Grammatical Number.** Decide: can a noun show number, is it implied, do plurals matter, is it more than plural, etc.
> 
> **Possession.** How do you say *It's hers* or *his*. That's what this is about.
> 
> **Grammatical Gender.** How you differentiate between different nouns. Doesn't *have* to be male/female/neuter, either.
> 
> **Ergativity.** This is to indicate what role nouns play in a sentence e.g. *She likes her* vs. *Her likes she*. Get it?
> It's optional for normal languages, with set word order, but if it's freeform, then you basically *need* this.
> 
> **Verb Conjugation.** This is possibly my favourite part of creating languages.
> You get to decide how verb roots change according to different rules, such as tense, gender, number, etc.

---

## Step 4: Consonant Vowel Clusters

> Decide on the syllable structure that's allowed, for example in Dothraki, the syllable structure is Doth-ra-ki, so you can't have something like *throne*.
> You can use the `CV` pattern to denote Consonants and Vowels respectively. For a specific letter, e.g. *r*, we have: `C(r)V(a)` this is just an example.

---

## Step 5: Word Formation

> Use your previously made rules to start mass-generating words in your vocabulary.
