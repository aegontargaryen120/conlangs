# Example Sentences

## 1. Basic Statements

### 1.1. You talk to me

**Language:**

> Vaelith lenys comarr

**Translation:** You talk to me.

| Word | Meaning / function |
|---|---|
| `Vaelith` | You; subject |
| `Lenys` | I / me; object |
| `Comarr` | Talk; recorded verb form |

**Grammar demonstrated:**
- Subject–Object–Verb (SOV) word order.
- The recorded form `comarr` in this sentence.

**Notes:** The exact derivation of `comarr` should be checked against the case and verb rules.

---

### 1.2. You are in the firestorm

**Language:**

> Vaelith fulmoreth conas

**Translation:** You are in the firestorm.

| Word | Meaning / function |
|---|---|
| `Vaelith` | You; subject |
| `Fulmoreth` | Firestorm; location |
| `Conas` | Are / be; recorded form |

**Grammar demonstrated:**
- Subject–Object–Verb ordering.
- The interaction between the conjugation of `Conos` and the *in* construction.

**Recorded derivation:**

1. `Conos` is the base form meaning “to be.”
2. Its present-simple conjugation is `cons`.
3. The *in* construction transforms this to `conas` in the established example.

**Notes:** Preserve `conas` as the established form.

## 2. Possession and Giving

### 2.1. I give you my lunch

**Language:**

> Lenys vaelith minuma tugs

**Translation:** I give you my lunch.

| Word | Meaning / function |
|---|---|
| `Lenys` | I; subject |
| `Vaelith` | You; recipient or object |
| `Minuma` | My meal / my lunch |
| `Tugs` | Give; recorded verb form |

**Grammar demonstrated:**
- Subject–Direct Object–Indirect Object–Verb (S–DO–IO–V) ordering, as specified in the language notes.
- The possessive suffix `-ma`.
- The use of a meal noun in an established sentence.

**Notes:**
- `Minu` means meal and can refer to breakfast, lunch, or dinner.
- `Minuma` means “my meal.”
- The exact derivation and grammatical classification of `tugs` should be documented in the verb reference.

## 5. Sentence Construction Template

The default sentence order is Subject–Object–Verb (SOV).

For sentences involving a subject, direct object, indirect object, and verb, the documented order is:

> Subject + Direct Object + Indirect Object + Verb

Use these patterns as starting points when constructing new sentences. Check the syntax and morphology references whenever a new grammatical feature is involved.
