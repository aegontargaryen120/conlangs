
# Phonology and Orthography Reference

## 1. Pronunciation

### 1.1. Established Pronunciation Rules

| Written form | Pronunciation |
|---|---|
| `Vae` | Like “vay” in *way*, but beginning with a hard /v/ |
| `a` | Like the vowel in *father*, never as in *cat* |
| `e` | Like the vowel in *entertaining* |
| `r` | Rolled /r/ |
| `c` | Hard /k/ sound |
| `th` | Like *th* in *thorn* |
| `y` | Like the long *ee* in *see* |

These are the currently established pronunciation rules. Other letters and letter combinations should retain their established pronunciations until further rules are defined.

## 2. Orthography

### 2.1. Writing System

- The language uses the Latin alphabet.
- The spelling conventions listed in this document take precedence over assumptions based on English pronunciation.
- The letter `c` always represents a hard /k/ sound under the current rules.
- The combination `th` represents the voiceless sound in *thorn*.

### 2.2. Vowel Spellings

| Spelling | Established pronunciation |
|---|---|
| `a` | As in *father* |
| `e` | As in *entertaining* |
| `y` | Long *ee* sound |
| `ae` in `Vae` | As in “vay” |

The pronunciation of other vowel combinations has not yet been fully specified.

### 2.3. Consonant Spellings

| Spelling | Established pronunciation |
|---|---|
| `c` | Hard /k/ |
| `r` | Rolled /r/ |
| `th` | As in *thorn* |

The pronunciations of other consonants and consonant combinations remain to be documented.

## 3. Consonant-Vowel Clusters

The following clusters are listed as permitted patterns in the current language specification.

| Pattern | Description | Example or status |
|---|---|---|
| `CVC` | Consonant–vowel–consonant | Listed as permitted |
| `VC` | Vowel–consonant | Listed as permitted |
| `CVCC` | Consonant–vowel–two consonants | Listed as permitted |
| `VV` | Two vowels | Listed as permitted |
| `CVVC` | Consonant–two vowels–consonant | `Vae` occurs in `Vaelith` |
| `VCCV` | Vowel–two consonants–vowel | Listed as permitted |

**Note:** These patterns are recorded from the existing specification. They have not yet been established as an exhaustive list of all possible syllable structures.

## 4. Existing Word Examples

These words illustrate the language's current spelling style.

| Word | Meaning | Recorded pattern |
|---|---|---|
| `Vaelith` | You | `CVVCVCC` |
| `Comare` | To talk | `CVCVCV` |
| `Cillientho` | Peak | `CVCCVVCCCV` |
| `Sirnyas` | Glory | `CVCCVVC` |

| `Oreth` | Storm | `VCVCC` |

The recorded patterns describe letter sequences, not necessarily the language's final syllabification rules.

## 5. Stress and Syllabification

Stress placement and complete syllabification rules have not yet been formally documented.

Do not assume that stress follows English conventions. Record the intended stress pattern once it has been established.
