# Nouns and Cases Reference

## 1. Cases

### 1.1. The “to” construction

When a word follows *to*, apply the rules below:

1. If the word ends in a vowel, replace that vowel with the preceding letter, provided the preceding letter is a consonant.
2. If the word does not end in a consonant–vowel (`CV`) pair, simply remove the final vowel.

These rules are recorded as written in the current specification. If both conditions appear to apply to a word, check the intended form rather than guessing.

| Base form | Meaning / role | Transformation |
|---|---|---|
| `Comare` | To talk | `comarr` in the sentence `Vaelith lenys comarr` (“You talk to me”) |

**Note:** The example `comarr` is an established sentence form. The general rule may need further examples to make its derivation unambiguous.

### 1.2. The “in” construction

The notes identify `-s` as the case ending associated with *in*, then give these spelling transformations:

| Word ending | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Vowel | Append the preceding consonant to the word | `comare` → `comarer` |
| Consonant | Append the preceding vowel to the word | `conos` → `conoso` |
| Consonant–consonant (`CC`) pair | Insert `a` between the final two consonants | Apply to a CC-final form |

The CC rule is an additional spelling rule. Apply it where relevant and preserve established examples.

#### Established sentence

> `Vaelith fulmoreth conas`

**Translation:** “You are in the firestorm.”

The intended derivation is:

1. `Conos` (“to be”) takes the present-simple conjugation: `cons`.
2. Applying the *in* construction to `cons` gives `conas`.

The sentence is described in the language notes as an irregular construction. Preserve `conas` as the established form.

## 2. Plurals

Verbs do not change for plural number. Nouns follow the rules below unless they are irregular.

| Noun ending | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Vowel | Append the second-last letter of the word, if the word ends in a vowel | `minu` → `minun` |
| Consonant, ending in a `VC` pair | Apply the same rule as for vowel-final nouns | Use the penultimate letter at the end |
| Consonant, ending in a `CC` pair | Find the first vowel in the word and append it to the end | Apply to CC-final nouns |

### Irregular nouns

Some nouns may be irregular. Record each irregular plural explicitly in the dictionary rather than forcing it to follow the general rule.

## 3. Possession

The suffix `-ma` means **“my”** and attaches to the noun.

| Base noun | Meaning | Possessive form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `minu` | Meal (breakfast, lunch, or dinner) | `minuma` | My meal |

The suffix `-ta` means **“theirs”** and attaches to the noun.

The suffix `-ia` means **“his/hers”** and attaches to the noun.

### Established sentence

> `Lenys vaelith minuma tugs`

**Translation:** “I give you my lunch.”

This example demonstrates the possessive suffix `-ma` in a sentence with the established S–DO–IO–V order.

## 4. Order of transformations

Case marking, pluralisation, and possession are distinct morphological operations. The current specification does **not** fully establish the order in which they combine.

When a noun needs more than one operation:

1. Preserve any established example.
2. Do not assume an affix order that has not been defined.
3. Record the combined form once its intended construction has been decided.
