# Syntax Reference

## 1. Basic Word Order

The default sentence structure is **Subject–Object–Verb (SOV)**.

The verb generally appears at the end of the sentence.

### Example

**Sentence:** You talk to me.

**Language:**

> Vaelith lenys comarr

**Recorded meanings:**
- `Vaelith` — You
- `Lenys` — I / me
- `Comare` — To talk; `comarr` appears in the established sentence

The example demonstrates the subject–object–verb pattern.

## 2. Direct and Indirect Objects

The more granular sentence structure is:

**Subject–Direct Object–Indirect Object–Verb (S–DO–IO–V).**

### Example

**Sentence:** I give you my lunch.

**Language:**

> Lenys vaelith minuma tugs

**Recorded meanings:**
- `Lenys` — I
- `Vaelith` — You
- `Minuma` — My meal / my lunch
- `Tugs` — Give; the form used in this sentence

The sentence demonstrates the recorded ordering of the subject, direct object, indirect object, and verb.

## 3. Adjective Placement

Adjectives appear **before the nouns they modify**.

Adjective forms remain unchanged regardless of tense, number, word order, or other grammatical factors.

### Examples

- `guri minu` — good meal
- `nira minu` — bad meal
- `mina Fulma` — small fire
- `fira Molga` — big ice

These are illustrative adjective–noun phrases, not complete sentences.

## 4. Possession

The suffix `-ma` expresses “my” and attaches to a noun.

### Example

> minuma

**Meaning:** My meal.

The possessive form appears in the established sentence:

> Lenys vaelith minuma tugs

**Translation:** I give you my lunch.

## 5. Case and Word Order

The language has documented transformations associated with the English constructions *to* and *in*.

These transformations affect word forms. They should be distinguished from the order in which sentence constituents appear.

### 5.1. The “to” construction

An established example is:

> Vaelith lenys comarr

**Translation:** You talk to me.

The word `comarr` is the form recorded in this sentence. Consult `nouns-and-cases.md` for the current transformation rules.

### 5.2. The “in” construction

An established example is:

> Vaelith fulmoreth conas

**Translation:** You are in the firestorm.

The form `conas` reflects the documented interaction between the conjugation of `Conos` and the *in* construction.

Consult `conjugation-reference.md` and `nouns-and-cases.md` for the relevant rules.

## 6. Questions

Rules for forming questions have not yet been established.

When designing questions, decide whether the language uses:
- A change in word order.
- A question particle.
- A change in intonation.
- Question words in a particular sentence position.

These are possible design choices, not existing rules.

## 7. Negation

Rules for negation have not yet been established.

The language needs a defined way to express meanings such as:
- I do not talk.
- You are not here.
- I do not want the meal.

The placement and behaviour of any negative word or particle should be documented once decided.

## 8. Commands and Requests

Rules for imperative sentences have not yet been established.

Future examples may include:
- Come here.
- Help me.
- Do not go.
- Please talk to me.

Do not assume that commands use the ordinary SOV structure until the imperative system has been defined.
